# Drums Practice — the curriculum

From your first beat to pro rhythms, on Move's pads with two hands. This page is the plan of the
Path: how it is built, what every step asks and why, and what is still to come. The README says
how to use the module, and [Finding your way](finding-your-way.md) how to get about it; this says
how the course is meant to teach.

**Finger drumming, not a drum kit.** Everything is played with two hands on the pads, so
"independence" means two hands and fingers, never hands and feet, and stick technique — bounce,
buzz, rebound — stays out. What the course trains is rhythm, reading, coordination between the
hands, dynamics, time and the ear.

## How to use the path

- **Continue**, the top row of the list, opens the next step of the stage you are in, from the
  track you have left longest, so a session walks Time, Hands, Grooves and Ear in turn. When
  something you learned is due again, it offers one review, then one new step, in turn.
- **Shift + click** a row for its options: *I know this: skip* passes a step by (on Continue, the
  step it would open), *I know it all: skip* a whole stage, track or groove, *Undo skip* takes
  it back, *Start the path here* moves the start, *Test out: checkpoint* plays a stage's exam.
- A stage folder lists its places in the order a lesson takes them — orient, hear, pattern,
  apply, show: **Lessons**, **Ear**, **Time**, **Hands**, **Grooves**, **Electives**, and the
  **Checkpoint** last.
- A step sets **Speed** to what it asks: a drill new to you arrives slow, at 60–85% of its tempo,
  and comes back faster in a later stage. Clean at the step's target is enough; the ✓ on the drill
  itself still means clean at 100%.
- Each **stage** ends on a **checkpoint**, a piece of music. Pass it and the next stage opens —
  you can take it at any time, which is how someone who can already play tests out of a stage.
- Before a new idea comes a **lesson card**: a screen or two on what it is and where you hear it.
  Click starts the practice, read or not; *Settings › Path › Intros* turns them to `always` or
  `off`, and every stage's **Lessons** row shows them again — as does **Menu** on a row of the path.
- **Settings › Path** decides what Continue offers: tracks and styles on or off, the full ladder of
  a groove or only L0 and its top rung, and the stage to start at.
- Stuck? After three takes that did not count the result suggests an easier way in (Speed down,
  or L0); after six the step is parked for a day and Continue offers something else.

## Legend

◆ in the module today · ★ planned: named in the path already, and left out of it until written.
Steps marked ★ appear on the device the day their drill ships, with no change to the path.

## Principles of the path

- **Finger drumming.** Two hands on pads: "independence" is two-hand and finger independence;
  stick-only technique (bounce, buzz, rebound) stays out. Rudiments train the hands' alternation,
  doubles and grace notes — on pads, the left/right split.
- **Stages × tracks.** Seven stages, each one musical world. Inside a stage four tracks
  interleave — **Time** (pulse, reading, the Clock), **Hands** (patterns, rudiments, dynamics),
  **Grooves** (beats, fills, phrases), **Ear**. Continue recommends steps of the current stage only.
- **Core and electives.** A stage's *core* is the few steps that teach its idea (8–14). Electives
  are more of the same idea in other styles — never required, chosen by taste.
- **One new thing per step.** Every core step below names its single novelty (*New:*). Tempo is
  a novelty too: a drill that is new arrives slow and comes back faster in a later stage (spiral).
- **Achievable first.** A first appearance asks 60–85% of the written tempo; every groove starts
  at L0; the first ✓ comes within five minutes of opening the module.
- **Checkpoints are music**, not lists: a phrase or a groove under the Clock.
- ★ **Three marks**: ✓ Learned (clean at the step's target), ★ Solid (clean at 100% on Strict
  *tight*, dynamics ≥ 90%), ★★ Pro (clean on tight at pro tempo: 130%, rudiments 160%).
- ★ **A lesson, not a list.** Continue becomes ~20 minutes: warm-up (a learned Hands step at 80%)
  → two new core steps from different tracks → due reviews (≤3) → play-out (a learned groove
  or phrase).
## The path in detail

Notation: counts `1 e & a`; `→ 80%` = step target as share of the drill's written tempo (the
spiral); ◆ existing drill, ★ new (specified in *Exercise catalogue*). "Pass" is the drill's normal
✓ rule (90% right, σ in window) at the target unless a `dyn` target is named. Electives in one
line each. *Learner:* what the student is likely thinking. *Why:* what the step does for them.

### Stage 0 — Hello, kit (1–2 sessions)
Goal: find kick, snare, hat without searching; play a steady beat at 70.

0.1 **Guess: drum** ◆ — pool KK·SN·HH, round 10. *New:* the staff/grid ↔ pad map.
*Learner:* "Kick bottom-left, and it's the bottom of the staff too — that's logical."
*Why:* every later chart assumes this map; three voices make it a three-minute first ✓.

0.2 **Hear: drum** ◆ — same pool. *New:* sound ↔ pad.
*Learner:* "Thump, crack, tick." *Why:* from now on they hear which drum they got wrong, so
the azure/red pads become something they also hear.

0.3 **Quarters** ◆ `subdiv:quarters` → 60 bpm. *New:* the result screen.
*Learner:* "Too easy… but σ 28 ms? I thought I was steady."
*Why:* they learn to read right %, σ and ±ms on the simplest material, so later feedback is
understood rather than feared — and they discover that steadiness is the real game.

0.4 **Reading I** ★ `read:1` → 70 — quarters and quarter rests on the snare, beat 1 always played,
fresh each time. *New:* rests.
*Learner:* "The gap is harder than the notes; I want to fill it."
*Why:* counting through silence is the base of all reading; a rest is a beat you still feel.

0.5 **First beat** ★ L0 → L3 → 70 — HH on 1 2 3 4, KK 1 and 3, SN 2 and 4. *New:* two hands at once.
*Learner:* L0 "I can find it slowly." L2 "Hat and kick together — it flams." L3 "I'm playing a beat!"
*Why:* every kick and snare lands *with* a hat, so coordination is pure unison, the easiest kind;
L0's "struck together" rule teaches the unison. The first real beat in session one.

0.6 **Checkpoint 0** — First beat L3 at 70, clean.
*Learner:* "Done — that was quick." *Why:* the exam they pass on day one sets the rhythm of the
course: practise → checkpoint → next world.

Electives: Motown ◆ → 60% (quarters only, snare on every beat); Two-beat ◆ → 50% (quarters on
ride, hat foot on 2 & 4 — jazz flavour without swing); Pick: drum ◆ (3-voice pool).

### Stage 1 — Eighth notes (1–2 weeks)
Goal: 8th-note beats with kick on any 8th, a 2-beat fill landing on a crash, one silent bar.

1.1 **Eighths** ◆ `subdiv:eighths` → 80. *New:* "1 &".
*Learner:* "Counting 1-&-2-& out loud helps." *Why:* the accent on each number keeps the pulse
audible inside a faster stream.

1.2 **Reading II-a** ★ `read:2a` → 80 — quarters and 8th pairs, no lone "&". *New:* 8ths on paper.
*Learner:* "Two flags = two hits in one beat." *Why:* note values before syncopation.

1.3 **Off-beats** ★ `offbeat:a` → 80 — KK on every beat, HH on every "&". *New:* the "&" alone.
*Learner:* "My hat wants to land on the kick." *Why:* first independence, but as alternation —
never together — the gentlest kind; it is the disco/house hat they know from records.

1.4 **Doubles in 8ths** ★ `hands:RRLL:8` → 80. *New:* two in a row per hand.
*Learner:* "My left is lazier." *Why:* finger drumming needs two equal hands; doubles expose the
weak one, and sticking is scored so the habit cannot hide.

1.5 **Eighth-note beat** ★ L0 → L3 → 80 — HH 8ths, KK 1 and 3, SN 2 and 4. *New:* hat in 8ths only.
*Learner:* "Only the hat changed." *Why:* exactly one change from First beat.

1.6 **Rock backbeat** ◆ L0 → L4 → 90% (83). *New:* a kick on "&" (3&).
*Learner:* "That & kick trips me." *Why:* the first syncopated kick, still under a hat stroke, so
the ear has an anchor.

1.7 **Kick variations** ★ `kickvar:8` → 80 — HH 8ths, SN 2 and 4, kick on 1 plus one more 8th
that moves through all seven positions, bar by bar. *New:* kick choice as a system.
*Learner:* "After this every rock beat is just a kick pattern." *Why:* coverage instead of
memorising grooves — it transfers to beats they have never seen.

1.8 **Pop four-floor** ◆ L0 → L4 → 80% (90). *New:* open hat on 4&, kick on every beat.
*Learner:* "The open hat makes it sound like a song." *Why:* new colour, familiar rhythm.

1.9 **Reading II-b** ★ `read:2b` → 80 — lone "&"s and rests on beats. *New:* light syncopation.
*Learner:* "I lose 1 when it starts on a rest." *Why:* they now have off-beats (1.3) in the hands,
so reading them is recognition, not invention.

1.10 **Phrase I** ★ `phrase:4:fill8-2` → 80 — three bars Eighth-note beat, bar 4: beats 1–2 groove,
3 & 4 & = SN SN HT LT, crash + kick on the next 1. *New:* a 4-bar form, a fill.
*Learner:* "My hands leave the hat and I lose where 1 is — the crash tells me I made it."
*Why:* songs are phrases; the fill is the reward and the crash the landmark.

1.11 **Clock: gap 1** ★ `clock:gap1` on Quarters → 80 — 1 bar click, 1 bar silent. *New:* silence.
*Learner:* "The click vanishes and I panic — did I rush?" *Why:* internal time in small doses;
the header's drift shows they were closer than they feared.

1.12 **Hear: subdivision** ◆ pool quarters/eighths. *Learner:* "Easy." *Why:* the ear confirms
what the hands just learned; a fast ✓ between harder steps.

1.13 **Pick: drum** ◆ full kit. *New:* toms, crash, open hat by ear — they appear in fills now.

1.14 **Checkpoint 1** — Phrase I at 90, clean, then Clock gap 1, clean.

Electives: Straight eights ◆; Halftime ballad ◆ (68, crash + tom fill — a musical reward);
Rock with fill ◆; Disco ◆ (hat on beats, open hat on "&"); Cha-cha ◆ → 80%; Reggae one-drop ◆
(kick and snare only on 3 — "where is one?"); Paradiddle in 8ths ★ `hands:RLRRLRLL:8`.

### Stage 2 — Sixteenths and dynamics (2–3 weeks)
Goal: 16th positions on any voice, accents and ghosts on purpose, two silent bars.

2.1 **Sixteenths** ◆ → 85% (68). *New:* "1 e & a".
*Learner:* "Four per beat, my hands fly." *Why:* the grid every later groove sits on.

2.2 **16th partials** ★ `partials:&` → `partials:a` → `partials:e` → `partials:mix`, each → 70 — KK on
every beat, SN only on the named partial. *New:* one partial at a time.
*Learner:* "The 'a' feels like a pickup; the 'e' right after the kick is the hardest."
*Why:* every 16th groove is a combination of these four spots; learned singly, combinations
become recognition. Ordered easy (&) → hard (e).

2.3 **Reading III-a** ★ `read:3a` → 70 — 8ths and full 16th groups. **Reading III-b** ★ `read:3b` → 70
— the four 16th cells (1-e-&, 1-&-a, 1-e-a, 1-a). *New:* 16th notation.
*Learner:* "Beams! Two beams = 16ths." *Why:* the cells are the vocabulary of funk.

2.4 **Single strokes** ◆ `single-stroke-roll` → 75% (60) · **Doubles** ◆ `double-stroke-roll` → 70% ·
**Paradiddle** ◆ `single-paradiddle` → 75% · **Five-stroke roll** ◆ → 70%. *New:* the PAS forms in 16ths.
*Learner:* "Same as Stage 1 — just twice as many." *Why:* the patterns are already in the hands
from 8ths; only density changes.

2.5 **Accents I** ★ `accent:8` → 80, `dyn 70` — 8ths alternating, one accent per bar moving 1 → & →
2 → …. **Accents II** ★ `accent:16` → 70, `dyn 70` — the accent moves through e/&/a.
*New:* loud on purpose, then soft again.
*Learner:* "I hit harder — and the next one is loud too." *Why:* an accent is a contrast; the
stroke *after* it is the skill. With dynamics scored apart, the take still counts while they work
on the sound.

2.6 **Ghost control** ★ `ghost:1` → 70, `dyn 70` — SN accents on 2 and 4, ghosts on the "a" before.
*New:* soft on purpose.
*Learner:* "Too soft and the pad doesn't fire." *Why:* they find the floor of their pads;
ghosts are what makes the next grooves breathe.

2.7 **House** ◆ L0 → L4 → 80% (99). *New:* ghost hat on "a" in a groove.
*Learner:* "This sounds like a record." *Why:* off-beats (1.3) + "a" partial (2.2) + ghost (2.6).

2.8 **Boom bap** ◆ L0 → L4 → 90% (79). *New:* kick on 2&, ghost snare on 3a.
2.9 **Electro** ◆ L0 → L4 → 75% (96). *New:* a 16th kick (2a).
*Learner:* "The kick lands between hats." *Why:* first kick that has no hat to hide under.

2.10 **Kick variations 16** ★ `kickvar:16` → 70 — like 1.7 over 16 positions.

2.11 **Sixteenth funk** ◆ L0 → L4 → 85% (82). *New:* two 16th kicks (1a, 2&) with ghosts.
*Learner:* "It's partials 2.2 in the foot." *Why:* the stage's ideas in one groove.

2.12 **Phrase II** ★ `phrase:4:fill16-1` then `phrase:4:fill16-2` → 80. *New:* 16th fills.

2.13 **Clock: gap 2** ★ on the Eighth-note beat → 80.

2.14 **Hear: subdivision** ◆ pool quarters/eighths/sixteenths · **Pick: groove** ◆ pool = grooves
they have played.

2.15 **Checkpoint 2** — Sixteenth funk at 100% clean (dynamics shown, not required) + Clock gap 2.

Electives: Disco ◆; Minimal ◆; Dubstep ◆ → 70%; Punk ◆ → 70%; Halftime ballad ◆; Paradiddle at 100%.

### Stage 3 — Ghosts, flams, independence (3–4 weeks)
Goal: ghost-note grooves with real dynamics, reading a kick line under an ostinato, flams and
drags, 8-bar form.

3.1 **Switching 8ths↔16ths** ★ `switch:8-16` → 80. *New:* changing grid mid-line.
*Learner:* "The change is where I wobble." *Why:* music changes subdivision; practising them one at
a time never trains the seam.

3.2 **Kick reading** ★ `kickread:8` → 80, then `kickread:16` → 70 — HH 8ths + SN 2 and 4 fixed,
the kick reads a fresh line each time. *New:* reading one limb while two keep time.
*Learner:* "My hat stops when the kick gets busy." *Why:* the core independence skill; after it a
written groove is read, not memorised.

3.3 **Snare variations** ◆ `groove` (Groove variation) → 90. *New:* the snare moves, kick and hat fixed.

3.4 **Ghost-note funk** ◆ L0 → L4 → 90% (85). *New:* 16th hats with ghosts between backbeats.
*Learner:* "Right 95%, dyn 40% — ok, the notes are there, now the sound." *Why:* the separate
dynamics score lets them clear the step and keep improving the feel without being blocked.

3.5 **Linear funk** ◆ L0 → L4 → 90%. *New:* no two voices ever together.
*Learner:* "It's a melody passed between hands." *Why:* linear thinking is native to finger drumming.

3.6 **Flam** ◆ → 80% · **Flam tap** ◆ → 80% · **Drag** ◆ → 80% · **Drag tap** ◆ → 80%. *New:* grace notes.
*Learner:* "A flam is two notes very close — the other hand first." *Why:* on pads a grace is a
measured early stroke; written open, closed as tempo rises.

3.7 **Singles, Doubles, Paradiddle** ◆ → 100% (the spiral returns).

3.8 **Techno four** ◆ L0 → L4 → 80% (102) · **Breakbeat** ◆ L0 → L4 → 80%. *New:* ghosted 16th hats
at club tempo; a 2-bar break.

3.9 **Funk with toms** ◆ → 85%. *New:* tom fill inside a 2-bar groove.

3.10 **Phrase III** ★ `phrase:8` → 85 — 8 bars, 1-beat fill in bar 4, 2-beat fill in bar 8.
*Learner:* "I have to count bars." *Why:* song form; the header's bar count helps.

3.11 **Clock: gap 4** ◆ (today's Clock) on Rock backbeat → 90%.

3.12 **Echo I** ★ `echo:8` (engine work) — hear a bar, play it back.

3.13 **Checkpoint 3** — Ghost-note funk at 100%, clean, **dynamics ≥ 80%**.

Electives: Punk ◆ 100%; Reggae one-drop ◆; Minimal ◆; Dub techno ◆; Detroit ◆; Drum & bass ◆ → 65%;
Seven-stroke ◆; Nine-stroke ◆; Lesson 25 ◆.

### Stage 4 — Triplets, shuffle, swing (3–4 weeks)
Goal: switch straight ↔ triplet feel, shuffle, jazz ride with the click on 2 and 4, 6/8 and 12/8.

4.1 **Triplets** ◆ → 80% (72). *New:* "1-trip-let".
*Learner:* "Three feels rounder." *Why:* the second family of time.

4.2 **Switching 8ths↔triplets** ★ `switch:8-t` → 70. *New:* changing family.
*Learner:* "My triplets slide back into 8ths." *Why:* the most common timing fault in grooves.

4.3 **Reading triplets** ★ `read:t` → 70 — triplets with rests, incl. the shuffle cell (1 _ let).

4.4 **Swing ratio** ★ `swing:58` → `swing:66` → 90 — 8th hats, the "&" moved later.
*New:* swing as a continuum.
*Learner:* "That's the swing knob on my MPC." *Why:* connects to what finger drummers already
use; 66% is the triplet shuffle of 4.5.

4.5 **Shuffle** ◆ L0 → L4 → 90%. *New:* hat on 1 and "let", SN 2 and 4.
4.6 **Half-time shuffle** ◆ → 90%. *New:* snare only on 3.
4.7 **12/8 blues** ★ → felt pulse 60. *New:* 12/8 counted as four groups of three.
4.8 **6/8 ballad** ★ → felt pulse 50. *New:* six 8ths felt in two.
*Learner:* "So 12/8 is a shuffle written honestly." *Why:* the same feel seen in three notations.

4.9 **Single-stroke four** ◆ · **seven** ◆ · **Triple-stroke roll** ◆ · **Flam accent** ◆ ·
**Swiss army triplet** ◆ · **Paradiddle-diddle** ◆ · **Double paradiddle** ◆ — each → 80%.
*Why:* triplet-based rudiments wait until triplets are known (fixing critique 2).

4.10 **Jazz ride** ◆ (fixed) L0 → L4 → 70% (92). *New:* ride 1, 2-a, 3, 4-a; hat foot on 2 and 4.
*Learner:* "Jazz! The 'a' before 3 is the tricky one." *Why:* the shuffle cell on the ride.

4.11 **Clock: click on 2 & 4** ★ `clock:24` on Jazz ride → 70%. *New:* the click as backbeat.
*Learner:* "I keep hearing the click as 1 and 3 and flip." *Why:* the jazz way to feel time.

4.12 **Two-beat** ◆ → 80% · **Jazz waltz** ◆ → 80%. *New:* swing in 3.

4.13 **Sextuplets** ◆ → 80% · **Trap hats** ◆ → 75%. *New:* 16th triplets (the trap roll).

4.14 **Straight or swung?** ★ quiz · **Which meter?** ★ quiz (4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 12/8) ·
**Hear: subdivision** ◆ full pool.

4.15 **Checkpoint 4** — Jazz ride at 100% (132), clean, with the click on 2 & 4.

Electives: UK garage ◆ (swung 16ths); Afro-Cuban 6/8 ◆ → 75%; Up-tempo swing ◆ → 50%.

### Stage 5 — Clave and the world (4–6 weeks)
Goal: hear and keep the clave, play the Latin and Afro grooves built on it, 3 over 2.

5.1 **Son clave 3-2** ★ → 90 — SN on 1, 2&, 4 | 2, 3; KK on 1 and 3 as anchor. *New:* a 2-bar key.
*Learner:* "Three then two — it's a rhythm that leans." *Why:* everything in this stage is
organised around it.

5.2 **Son clave 2-3** ★ · **Rumba clave 3-2** ★ (third note on 4&). *New:* direction; one note moved.
*Learner:* "Same pattern started in the middle." *Why:* the band decides the direction; they must
hear both.

5.3 **Clave direction?** ★ quiz. *Why:* ear check right after the hands learned it.

5.4 **Hemiola** ★ `poly:hemiola` → 70 — 8th triplets, every second one accented. **3 over 2** ★
`poly:3:2` → 60 — KK on beats, SN on quarter-note triplets. *New:* two pulses at once.
*Learner:* "Accenting the triplets made the 3 appear by itself." *Why:* the polyrhythm is
built from a grid they already own, not counted in the abstract.

5.5 **Bossa nova** ◆ → 75% · **Samba** ◆ → 75% · **Songo** ◆ → 75% · **Mozambique** ◆ → 70% — each L0 → L4.
*New per groove:* dotted-16th kick (bossa, samba); hat foot + displaced kick (songo); bell on the toms (mozambique).
*Learner:* "L0 again — good, these are dense." *Why:* L0 removes tempo pressure exactly where
density is highest.

5.6 **Flamacue** ◆ · **Flam paradiddle** ◆ · **Single ratamacue** ◆ · **Double drag tap** ◆ ·
**Six-** and **Ten-stroke** ◆ — each → 80%. **Round the kit** ★ `orchestrate:paradiddle` — R on
the toms, L on the snare. *New:* rudiments as sound, not exercise.

5.7 **Clock: gap 4** ◆ on Bossa nova → 75%.

5.8 **Checkpoint 5** — Songo at 90% clean + Clave direction ≥ 90%.

Electives: Cha-cha ◆; Afrobeat ◆; Second line ◆; Mambo ◆; Tribal techno ◆; Detroit ◆;
Eleven/Thirteen/Fifteen/Seventeen-stroke ◆; Afro-Cuban 6/8 ◆ at 100%.

### Stage 6 — Pro (open-ended)
Goal: odd meters, groupings, displacement, feel on purpose, pro tempos on Strict tight.

6.1 **Counting 5/4, 7/8 (2+2+3), 7/8 (3+2+2)** ★ `odd:*` → 90 — 8ths with accents on group starts.
**Groove in 5/4** ★, **Groove in 7/8** ★. *New:* bars that are not 4.
*Learner:* "Counting 1-2 1-2 1-2-3 is easier than counting to 7." *Why:* groups, not totals.

6.2 **Groupings** ★ `group:3`, `group:5` → 70, `dyn 80` — 16ths accented every 3/5, resolving after 3/5 bars.
*Learner:* "The accents drift across the bar line and come home." *Why:* the modern-fill vocabulary.

6.3 **Displacement** ★ `displace:rock-backbeat:1`, `:2` — the beat shifted by one/two 16ths, L0 first.
*Learner:* "A beat I know became alien." *Why:* proves they know *where* each note is, not just its shape.

6.4 **4 over 3** ★ `poly:4:3` → 60.

6.5 **Clock: off-beat click** ★ `clock:off` and **one click per 2 bars** ★ `clock:bar`.
*Learner:* "The off-beat click is brutal." *Why:* the hardest test of internal time.

6.6 **Feel: behind / ahead** ★ `feel:behind` (mean +10..+25 ms), `feel:ahead` (−5..−15 ms) on a
learned groove, σ ≤ tight. *New:* placement on purpose (engine work).
*Learner:* "I can make the same beat lazy or urgent." *Why:* what separates a pro from a metronome.

6.7 **Pro tempo** — Drum & bass ◆, Hard techno ◆, Dubstep ◆, Up-tempo swing ◆ at 100%; every
learned drill at its ★★ target. Remaining flams & drags ◆ (pataflafla, inverted flam tap, flam
drag, flam paradiddle-diddle, single flammed mill, single dragadiddle, drag paradiddle 1/2,
double/triple ratamacue) → 100%.

6.8 **Echo IV** ★ (2 bars, mixed) · **Rushing or dragging?** ★ quiz.

6.9 **Checkpoint 6** — a Stage-6 groove at 100% on Strict tight with the off-beat click.
## Dynamics: their own score, their own feedback

The verdict answers **"did you play the notes, in time?"**; dynamics answer **"how did they
sound?"** Two questions, two numbers. A take can be CLEAN at 100% with dynamics at 30%.

- **What counts as right** (`takeAccuracy`): right drum, right hand (strict sticking), on time.
  Dynamic errors leave it — also in L0's right-first-time and on the Ladder. A missed note is
  not judged for dynamics either: one mistake, one error.
- **The dynamics score** (`scoring.mjs`): every *hit* accent or ghost that is judged (existing
  `dynNeed` rule: Dynamics on, ≥3 normal strokes to compare with) earns credit for how far it
  got: accent `(vel − ref) / lift`, ghost `(ref − vel) / drop`, clamped 0..1, `ref` = the running
  median it was judged against. Score = mean credit × 100, accents and ghosts kept apart. An
  accent halfway to its target earns half — movement is visible before it is right. `dynOk`
  (azure pad, review rings) stays binary. Fewer than 4 judged notes → `dyn --`.
- **Feedback**, one line each, no new screen: result side lines keep `94% right` and `s12 +3ms`;
  the result row shows `dyn 30%` right-aligned whenever judged; its left part keeps the reason a
  take did not count, and on a CLEAN take with dynamics < 90% becomes the dynamics advice
  (`16 soft 74<83`, `5 loud ghosts 61>48`) — one correction at a time: notes first, then sound.
  L0's row: `16 stacks … dyn 30%`. Live feedback unchanged.
- **Settings**: Dynamics off no longer eases a take; it hides the score and earns no ★.
- **Progress & stats**: history entry carries dyn %; `item.dyn` = best dyn % of a standard clean
  take at tempo (progress v2, see *Progress v2*); stats record `dp`, `gv`, `gn`.
  The drill's Progress chart draws dynamics as a dotted second line.
- **Where it pays off**: ★ needs dynamics ≥ 90%; a path step can carry `dyn` (the dynamics
  studies and the Stage-3 checkpoint do). Everything else ticks on notes and time alone.
## Lesson cards — a short explanation before the practice

**What.** A card is 1–2 screens (≤ 7 lines × ~21 characters on the 128×64 display) naming one
concept: what it is, where you hear it, what to listen for. One card per *concept*, not per drill,
so a concept is explained once and the cards read as a thread through the course. Each stage
also has a stage card (what this world is, what you can do at the end).

**When.** Opening a path step whose card is unseen shows the card before the Ready screen; a
stage card shows when Continue enters a new stage; the checkpoint has its card. Library drills
opened outside the path show none — the library is free play.

**Skipping is one press.** On a card: **Click** = start (to the Ready screen, card marked seen);
**Jog** = next/previous page; **Play** = hear the drill (goes to Ready and listens — the same
"Play listens" as everywhere); **Back** = out to the list. *Settings › Path › Intros*: `new`
(default, each card once) / `always` / `off`. Each stage folder opens on a **Lessons** row listing
its cards to re-read at any time, and a stage's action sheet offers them too.

## Keeping it achievable — anti-frustration rules

- **First ✓ in five minutes** (0.1), first beat in session one (0.5).
- **One novelty per core step**, named on the step; tempo counts as a novelty.
- **First appearance at 60–85%**; the same drill returns at 100% one or two stages later.
- **L0 before every groove**; dynamics never block a ✓ (except steps that are *about* dynamics).
- **Stuck rule** (new): after 3 not-clean takes of a step the result suggests the easier way in
  (`try 85%` via knob 8, or `L0`, or `Study`); after 6, Continue *parks* the step for a day and
  recommends another track — "come back tomorrow" is how teachers handle a plateau. A parked
  step is still playable; it shows `~`.
- **Stage length** is capped by core size (8–15 steps), so a checkpoint is never far away.
## Flexibility — skipping and switching

**Before this path:** nothing was locked; any drill can be played from the library and counts on the path;
Skip next exists; a higher rung ticks the lower ones; Click goes on even after a failed take.
**But:**
- *Skip next* skips only the step Continue would pick — to skip a particular step you must first
  make it Continue's choice; one press per step (a groove = 5 rungs; all of Latin ≈ 50 presses).
- No un-skip except playing the step.
- No way to drop a track (e.g. no Ear) or a style (e.g. no jazz) — Continue keeps bringing them.
- No way to choose where to start; an experienced player clicks through or plays everything once.
- Switching track from a result meant Menu → Learning Path → the track.

**Now:**
1. **Settings › Path** (a group of the Settings folder, the last row of the list):
   - **Tracks** — Time / Hands / Grooves / Ear on/off (at least one stays on).
   - **Styles** — Rock & Pop / Funk / Latin / Jazz / Electronic / Techno on/off; decides which
     electives Continue offers and the Pick: groove pool. Core steps stay (a stage's idea is
     needed), but a core groove in a switched-off style is swapped for an elective of the same
     idea in a style that is on (each core groove step lists its alternatives).
   - **Rungs** — full ladder / short (L0 and L4; L1–L3 stay playable, not recommended).
   - **Start at** — Stage 0…6: Continue starts there; earlier stages stay open, nothing ticked.
   - **Intros** — `new` / `always` / `off` (lesson cards).
2. **Test out**: every stage's **Checkpoint**, its last row, plays its checkpoint at any time, and so
   does *Test out: checkpoint* on the stage's action sheet; pass it and the stage is through
   (Continue moves on; the drills keep their honest marks).
3. **Skipping from the action sheet** (Shift + click a row): *I know this: skip* on a step or on
   Continue, *I know it all: skip* on a stage, track or groove, *Undo skip* on anything skipped.
   Skipped steps close the step for the stage's progress but are never learned; `»` as today. The
   skip rows that used to stand among the steps are gone.
4. **Stage completion** = checkpoint passed, or tested out, or every core step of the tracks
   that are on done/skipped. A stage cannot trap anyone.
5. **Switching from a result:** Click = next in this track (as today); Back to the list, then
   Continue picks the track left longest — unchanged, plus the parked rule.
6. Preferences live in `progress.json` (`progress.path`), so they survive updates with the progress.

## Design notes

The review this curriculum came from, kept so the reasons stay with the plan.

### What is good — keep it

L0 untimed first rung; rungs derived from one file (a voice never moves); scoring mean and σ
rather than hit count; accents judged against the player's own strokes; "clean at tempo" as the
one verdict; best never drops; nothing locked; interleaved tracks; spaced repetition; Click goes
on even after a failed take. The problems are in **sequencing, range and flexibility**, not engine.
### Critique — what was wrong before this path (by impact)

1. **Grooves are ordered by genre, not difficulty.** The track walks all 8 rock grooves × 5 rungs
   (~40 steps) before any funk; techno four-on-the-floor comes after jazz and Latin; up-tempo
   swing at 270 bpm before house. Motown and two-beat are quarter notes only, yet sit mid-list.
2. **No cross-track sequencing.** Continue round-robins four independent tracks: on day one a
   beginner gets Quarters, then *single-stroke roll in 16ths at 80*, then a groove in 8ths, before
   ever playing an eighth in Time. Single-stroke four/seven (triplet/sextuplet) sit in Rudiments
   unit 1, long before triplets appear in Time. The kit legend (Guess: drum) is reached fourth.
3. **The first beat is missing.** The first groove already has 8th hats and a kick on "&3". No
   quarter-note beat, no plain 8th beat with kick on 1 and 3.
4. **Reading has no note values and no rests.** Random 8ths/16ths on one drum at a fixed density:
   no quarters, no deliberate rests, no graded syncopation, no multi-voice reading.
5. **Coordination is barely trained.** One generator moves the snare over a fixed hat/kick. No
   kick reading under an ostinato — the skill between "knows five beats" and "can play anything".
6. **No fills, no form.** One fill drill. No 4/8-bar phrases, no crash on one.
7. **Dynamics gate the verdict and are never taught on their own.** `takeAccuracy`
   (`src/progress.mjs:146`) counts a soft accent or loud ghost as *not right* — for the 90% gate it
   costs what a missed note costs; in L0 it spoils "right first time" (`src/untimed.mjs`
   complete()); on a Ladder rung (share 1) one soft accent ends the climb. A student who plays
   every note on time sees NOT CLEAN. And accents/ghosts are never practised in isolation.
8. **Feel is absent.** Swing only as 12/8; no straight→swung continuum (the MPC swing knob every
   finger drummer knows); no laid-back/pushed playing — although the module measures the mean.
9. **Time is under-trained.** The Clock appears once, at the end of Time, on quarters only.
10. **The ceiling is low.** Learned = written tempo (rudiments 64–80 bpm), σ ≤ 60 ms regardless
    of subdivision. No odd meters, polyrhythm, groupings, displacement.
11. **Ear is thin and unfair.** Pick: groove asks about all 42 grooves including unheard ones;
    Hear: subdivision includes sextuplets from the start; no rhythm dictation.
12. **Errors in the material.** `jazz-ride.json` has the skip notes on 1a/3a (ride 0, 1, 1.5, 3,
    4, 4.5); the jazz ride is 1, 2, 2a, 3, 4, 4a — as `uptempo-swing.json` correctly has (0, 1.5,
    2.5, 3, 4.5, 5.5). Multiple bounce roll cannot be buzzed on a pad and duplicates singles.
13. **Skipping and switching are too coarse** — see *Flexibility* below.
